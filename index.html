01

LOCAL AI · ORCHESTRATION · RELIABILITY

Local Agent
Orchestrator

How do you let AI change real software without pretending model output is trustworthy?

I built a local-first platform that turns bounded tasks into reviewable work. It coordinates specialized roles, supplies only approved context, isolates edits, runs deterministic checks, records evidence, and stops at explicit approval boundaries.

What I discovered

Context is an engineered layer. More context can make a model worse. A context engine should select fresh, role-appropriate evidence within a strict budget and record exactly what each generation saw.

Semantic layers need different trust rules. Instructions, API definitions, retrieved evidence, model reasoning, tool calls, and approval are not interchangeable. Separating these layers makes failures visible and limits what a convincing answer can trigger.

Orchestration is mostly state management. Useful multi-agent work depends less on adding agents and more on durable identity, bounded ownership, recovery, independent review, and explicit handoffs.

02

GENERATIVE VIDEO · GPU PIPELINE · QUALITY CONTROL

Video Engine

Can one local machine turn a sourced story into a watchable short without giving up factual or visual control?

I built a resumable production pipeline for research, scripts, voice, recurring characters, generated scenes, captions, assembly, and machine-assisted QA. Every stage leaves an artifact. Every upload remains a human decision.

What I discovered

Consistency beats raw generation quality. Recurring character and location references, shot grammar, and still-first review make separate AI scenes feel like one story.

Exact facts need a different medium. Generated visuals are strong at atmosphere, not exact words or numbers. Code-drawn graphics and sourced scripts carry precision; generative models carry motion and mood.

Resumability is an efficiency booster. Saving every stage means one bad scene or chapter can be repaired without regenerating an entire video.

03

MULTI-MODEL CODING · ROUTING · OBSERVABILITY

Harness

Models fail differently. Why send every task to the same one or discover failure after the work is over?

I made a measured router and live dashboard around local, free, and frontier models. Workers can run in parallel; real task results rank them; dead streams and rate limits trigger bounded failover; privacy rules constrain where sensitive code can go.

What I discovered

The best model is task-dependent. Small local models can handle bounded edits cheaply and privately. Hard reasoning deserves escalation. Real completed tasks are a better router than marketing benchmarks.

Parallelism helps only when ownership is clear. Splitting independent research or implementation work saves time; overlapping edits and vague roles create coordination debt.

Efficiency includes failed work. Context pressure, empty generations, retries, and rate limits must be visible. Bounded failover turns hidden costs into measurable system behavior.

04

MARKET RESEARCH · TEMPORAL DATA · RISK GATES

Guarded Market
Automation

Financial automation is easy to demo and hard to make honest.

I built research and paper-trading paths where every claim must have been knowable at decision time. Risk can veto. Approvals bind to exact proposals. Fresh preflight runs before paper execution, and fills reconcile into a durable ledger.

What I discovered

Time is part of the data. A fact that existed historically was not necessarily available when a decision was made. Publication time, market sessions, and point-in-time universes prevent convincing hindsight.

Negative results create value. Preserving failed strategies and realistic costs prevents the system from repeatedly rediscovering attractive mistakes.

AI and authority must stay separate. A model can research and propose. Deterministic risk checks, exact approvals, fresh account state, and reconciliation control what happens next.
